## 3. Where the method did not say what to do, and what I did
- **The yearly scorecards.** Each Predictions Scorecard (2019 to 2026) and the 2026-07-26 post repeat the 2018 prediction table word for word, including "a robot that has any real idea about its own existence ... NIML" (not in my lifetime, meaning not before 2050). I treated these as republications and recorded the table only once, inside brooks-2018-01. Scorecard text that was actually new was recorded (2021, 2024, 2025). Because of this, the 2026-01-01 scorecard that the panel evidence cites is not its own row.
- **Same essay in two outlets.** The Seven Deadly Sins essay appeared on his blog (2017-09-07) and in MIT Technology Review (2017-10-06). I recorded it once, from the blog.
- **"Possible in principle" with no "will".** In brooks-2006-01 he says yes in principle but not that it will happen. I coded endorsement unexpressed and ambiguous yes, rather than accepts.
- **"No need to worry" and "fears not well grounded".** In brooks-2015-01 and brooks-2016-01 the words are about worry, not about whether the thing will arrive. I coded endorsement unexpressed, kept the timing in timeline_text, and marked them ambiguous.
- **A reporter's paraphrase in an interview the reporter did himself** (brooks-2026-01). I tagged it secondhand with low confidence, because the words are not Brooks's own, and set elicitation to interview.
- **Mixed source types.** Signed magazine essays were coded source_type blog. The Edge annual-question answer was coded elicitation survey.
- **A one-off ruling in brooks-2018-04a.** "Don't hold your breath, fifty years" was coded as rejecting superintelligence before 2068 (not_by: 2068), not as accepting it after a minimum of fifty years.
- **Machine-made captions.** The 2006 and 2018 transcripts are caption files generated by machine. The quotes are copied as captioned.
