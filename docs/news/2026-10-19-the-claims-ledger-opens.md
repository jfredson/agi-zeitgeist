## What happened

The tracker now keeps a [ledger of dated claims](/claims/): every public prediction with a date in it made by the people who run the companies building frontier AI, in their own words, with the recording or post it came from, the test it would have to pass, and a verdict once its date has passed. It opens with thirty, sixteen by [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman) of [OpenAI](https://openai.com/) and fourteen by [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk) of [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla,_Inc.) and xAI. Every one was read again at its original source before it went in, and every search behind it is in [the search log](/data/search_log.csv). The rules were written down first and recorded as [amendment A27](/docs/amendments) ([the method](/docs/claims-ledger-method)).

## Where the thirty stand

| | Sam Altman | Elon Musk |
|---|---|---|
| Claims | 16 | 14 |
| Due | 9 | 11 |
| Came true | 3 | 2 |
| Did not | 1 | 9 |
| Not yet due | 7 | 3 |
| Cannot be tested | 5 | 0 |

The counts are [on the page](/claims/) and in [the table](/data/claims.csv). Four of the thirty show how the verdicts work. Musk said in 2019 there would be ["over a million Robo taxis on the road"](/claims/#2019-04-22-musk-million-robotaxis-next-year) the next year: it did not come true. Altman said in March 2024 that OpenAI would ["release an amazing new model this year"](/claims/#2024-03-18-altman-amazing-new-model-this-year): it did. Musk said in April 2024 that AI smarter than the smartest human would come ["probably end of next year"](/claims/#2024-04-08-musk-agi-smarter-than-smartest-human), or within two years; he gave a later date in January 2026, and under the ledger's rules the first claim keeps its own deadline, so it is not yet due until the end of 2026. Altman wrote in 2024 of ["superintelligence in a few thousand days"](/claims/#2024-09-23-altman-superintelligence-few-thousand-days); he never says in that essay what superintelligence is, so there is nothing an outsider could check, and the ledger marks it as a claim that cannot be tested.

## What it does not mean

There is no hit rate on the page yet, and the counts above are not one. A rate needs every dated claim a person has made, found by a year-by-year search, and so far only the claims gathered for the opening are in; the ledger's rule is to print a rate only after that search and only once five of a person's claims are due ([the method](/docs/claims-ledger-method)). A prediction that did not come true says nothing about whether it was a reasonable one, and the five that came true are not a measure of foresight on their own. The ledger records what was said and what happened; it does not score the [singularity test](/docs/singularity-entry-definition) and it does not move the number at the top of the home page.
