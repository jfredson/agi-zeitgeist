## What happened

The tracker now keeps a [ledger of dated claims](/claims/): every public prediction with a date in it made by the people who run the companies building frontier AI, in their own words, with the recording or post it came from, the test it would have to pass, and a verdict once its date has passed. It holds 325 claims from fifteen people. Every one was read again at its original source before it went in, and every search behind it is in [the search log](/data/search_log.csv). The rules were written down first and recorded as [amendment A27](/docs/amendments) ([the method](/docs/claims-ledger-method)).

Who counts is set by [amendment A30](/docs/amendments): the chief executive, founder-leader or chair of a company that builds frontier AI models or the chips they are trained on, or its named chief scientist, and only for what they said while they held that role. The fifteen are [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman) of [OpenAI](https://openai.com/); [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk) of [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla,_Inc.) and [xAI](https://x.ai/), now called SpaceXSI; [Dario Amodei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dario_Amodei) of [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/); [Jensen Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang) of [Nvidia](https://www.nvidia.com/); [Demis Hassabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demis_Hassabis) and [Shane Legg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Legg) of [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/); [Mustafa Suleyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Suleyman), who co-founded DeepMind and now runs [Microsoft AI](https://microsoft.ai/); [Ilya Sutskever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Sutskever), formerly OpenAI's chief scientist and now head of [Safe Superintelligence](https://ssi.inc/); [Yann LeCun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_LeCun), chief AI scientist at [Meta](https://www.meta.com/about/) until the end of 2025; [Liang Wenfeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang_Wenfeng) of [DeepSeek](https://www.deepseek.com/); [Arthur Mensch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Mensch) of [Mistral AI](https://mistral.ai/); [Kai-Fu Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai-Fu_Lee) of [01.AI](https://www.01.ai/), counted from 2023, when he founded it; [Mark Zuckerberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Zuckerberg) of Meta; [Sundar Pichai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundar_Pichai) of [Google](https://about.google/) and its parent Alphabet; and [Satya Nadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya_Nadella) of [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/). The last three are in the ledger only: they are not among the 37 people whose views the tracker's opinion record follows, and their words never enter it ([amendment A30](/docs/amendments)).

The counts are [on the page](/claims/) and in [the table](/data/claims.csv). A claim is due once its date has passed. That includes claims that cannot be tested, which is why a person's due count can be larger than their came-true and did-not counts together: 17 of the 108 due claims are past their date but could never be checked ([the method](/docs/claims-ledger-method)).

Four of the claims show how the verdicts work. Musk said in 2019 there would be ["over a million Robo taxis on the road"](/claims/#2019-04-22-musk-million-robotaxis-next-year) the next year: it did not come true. Altman said in March 2024 that OpenAI would ["release an amazing new model this year"](/claims/#2024-03-18-altman-amazing-new-model-this-year): it did. Musk said in April 2024 that AI smarter than the smartest human would come ["probably end of next year"](/claims/#2024-04-08-musk-agi-smarter-than-smartest-human), or within two years; he gave a later date in January 2026, and under the ledger's rules the first claim keeps its own deadline, so it is not yet due until the end of 2026. Altman wrote in 2024 of ["superintelligence in a few thousand days"](/claims/#2024-09-23-altman-superintelligence-few-thousand-days); he never says in that essay what superintelligence is, so there is nothing an outsider could check, and the ledger marks it as a claim that cannot be tested.

## What it does not mean

There is no hit rate on the page yet, and the counts above are not one. A rate needs every dated claim a person has made, found by a year-by-year search, and so far only the claims gathered for the opening are in; the ledger's rule is to print a rate only after that search and only once five of a person's claims are due ([the method](/docs/claims-ledger-method)). A prediction that did not come true says nothing about whether it was a reasonable one, and the 36 that came true are not a measure of foresight on their own ([the claims page](/claims/)). Some people simply make more dated predictions than others, so the number of claims beside a name says how often they put a date on the future, not how right they are. The ledger records what was said and what happened; it does not score the [singularity test](/docs/singularity-entry-definition) and it does not move the number at the top of the home page.

*Corrected 2026-10-19 after an independent check.* Altman's goal of "an automated AI research intern by September of 2026" was first entered as came true, on OpenAI's own word. A check that day found that OpenAI defined the intern only when it announced success, judged it by its own measurements, and never showed the stated "hundreds of thousands of GPUs", so the claim [cannot be tested](/claims/#2025-10-29-altman-automated-research-intern-by-september-2026). The counts were changed from five came true to four, and Altman's from three to two.

*Widened 2026-10-19 on John's ruling.* This post first covered the thirty claims by Altman and Musk that the ledger opened with. The same day John ruled that it should cover the industry's figureheads, fifteen people in all ([amendment A30](/docs/amendments)), and the post was rewritten to cover them once a separate check of every new claim against its source had passed. That check led to corrections, all made before the new claims went up. The same change settled when a claim that cannot be tested counts as due: from its own date, not from the day it was entered. Under that rule the original thirty now read 17 due rather than the 20 this post first gave, and Altman's 6 rather than 9; their verdicts did not change, and four of them came true ([the claims page](/claims/)).
