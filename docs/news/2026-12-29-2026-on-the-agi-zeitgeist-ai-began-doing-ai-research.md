In February 2026 the AI company [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) counted that its own AI was leading less than 1 percent of the work of building its next AI. By August the figure was 26 percent ([Anthropic's measurements](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)). The rise moved forward one part of a test I keep on this site for whether we are entering the [technological singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity): the part that asks whether AI is doing the work of building AI. That part now stands 52 percent of the way to the line where it would count as crossed, closer than any of the other parts ([where the test stands](/)).

The singularity is the point where machines improve so quickly that nobody can keep up. The test has four parts, each with a line (a level fixed in advance), and all four have to cross their lines and stay there for a year before I would say we are in it. At the end of 2026 the three parts that can be measured stand, on average, 32 percent of the way to their lines, and the answer to whether we are in the singularity is still "Not yet" ([where the test stands](/)). Here is what moved, what did not, and what 2027 could settle.

## AI started doing the research

One part of the test asks whether AI now does most of the work of making AI better. The only numbers on this come from the AI companies themselves, and Anthropic's are the ones this site scores: if Anthropic's AI leads half of its research work, the part counts as crossed. At 26 percent, the part stands 52 percent of the way to that line, and if the trend of 2026 holds, it crosses the line in 2027 ([where the test stands](/)).

The obvious doubt is that only the company can check its own count. In November an outside group published a partial check. [METR](https://metr.org/), a research group that tests AI systems, spent from August 12th to October 29th, 2026 studying Anthropic from the inside. Most of METR's staff concluded that Anthropic's AI was improving about twice as fast as it had in 2024 and 2025, and that perhaps 80 percent of the speed-up came from Anthropic using its own AI in its research ([METR's report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)). The forecaster Ajeya Cotra argued that METR's finding is a much bigger deal than it sounds, and [I wrote about her case in November](/news/2026-11-13-cotra-ai-nearly-doubling-ai-progress/). METR's report measures how fast Anthropic's research goes, not how much of the research AI does, so the report does not change the 26 percent. It does make Anthropic's own count harder to wave away.

## The best AI outgrew the measure of how fast AI improves

Another part asks whether AI is getting better faster and faster. It is scored on METR's measure of how long a job, in a skilled person's time, the best AI can finish on its own half the time. In April 2026 an early version of Anthropic's Claude Mythos reached about 17 hours, past the 16 hours METR says its tasks can measure reliably, and in September METR stopped updating its record of this measure ([METR's page](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). Over the last twelve months of METR's record, the length of job doubled every 121 days. That is fast, but this part of the test needs the doubling itself to keep getting quicker, down to 30 days, and over those twelve months the doubling did not get quicker, so the part stands 43 percent of the way to its line ([where the test stands](/)).

So the measure I chose for this part ran out before the year did. I asked METR in October whether a replacement is planned and have had no answer. Choosing the replacement is my decision, and the rules I set before METR stopped updating say any replacement has to be shown running side by side with METR's measure before it counts. I will write about it when I decide.

## The economy did not change gear

A third part is measured by people outside the AI world: whether the world economy grows far faster than it ever has. The line is 30 percent growth in a year. In 2025 the world economy grew 2.9 percent, about what it has done for decades ([the World Bank's figures](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG?locations=1W)). So the economy part stands at zero. Because all four parts must cross, the economy alone keeps the answer at "Not yet", however fast the other parts move. The fourth part, whether AI has become impossible to predict, still cannot be measured, because nobody has gathered the record of old forecasts that scoring it would need ([where the test stands](/)).

## What the leaders said

The leaders of the AI companies, meanwhile, named early dates. Of the 12 heads of AI companies on this site who have put a year on AGI (a machine that can do most of the thinking work a person can) or something beyond it, 8 name a year before 2030, which is the middle year of the latest guesses from the long-time thinkers among the 37 experts whose statements this site records ([the leaders' predictions](/claims/)). In August, Sam Altman of [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI) told a reporter that by the end of 2026 the company would have an internal system he would call AGI ([TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/08/26/openai-sam-altman-interview/)). In July, 1,386 employees of frontier AI companies asked the US government to support an international effort to build the means to deliberately set the pace of AI that develops AI ([their statement](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/)). And on September 29th, 2026, the White House told federal agencies to write "Super Intelligence" where they used to write "Artificial Intelligence" ([the order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/)).

## What the site found and changed

This site opened on September 30th, 2026. On October 8th I fixed every line in the test before scoring anything. On October 17th the number at the top of the home page became the average of how far each measurable part has come toward its line, with the slowest part named, and on October 19th a page began recording what the fifteen people who run the leading AI companies and chipmakers predict, beside what the long-time thinkers say. From October 26th every one of the 37 experts I follow has been searched for new statements every week. Each change, with its date and its reason, is in [the story so far](/about/).

## Coming up in 2027

**January 1st.** The year closes on 29 predictions on the leaders' page whose date is the end of 2026 ([the table](/data/claims.csv)). Among them: Dario Amodei of Anthropic said in February 2025 that by the end of 2026 AI might be close to the best human programmers at everything in coding ([Hard Fork](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhGUSIvsn_Y)); Mustafa Suleyman of Microsoft AI said in December 2025 he was "pretty certain" people would be buying Christmas presents through an AI agent by Christmas 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MUEKVoeeRoA)); and Elon Musk said in July 2024 that Tesla would supply its Optimus robots to outside customers in 2026 ([Tesla's call](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ktkCRVxTuEI)). Each gets a short note saying what happened.

**January.** The World Bank's [Global Economic Prospects](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/global-economic-prospects), published twice a year, should give its first estimate of how fast the world economy grew in 2026. The estimate will be the first sign of whether the economy part moved at all.

**Sometime in 2027.** Anthropic says it will keep publishing its measurements, with no date given ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)). If Anthropic's AI leads half of its research, the AI-research part of the test crosses its line, the first of the four parts to do so.

**April 26th to 28th.** The [International Conference on Learning Representations](https://iclr.cc/), one of the largest AI research conferences, holds its 2027 meeting, the first at which every paper must say how AI was used in the research ([its dates](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/Dates), [its rule](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors)). If anyone counts those statements, the count could be the first measure of how much research AI does that does not rest on a company's word.

**All year.** Several of the predictions on the leaders' page name 2027 for machines that match or beat people at most thinking work. Amodei wrote that AI smarter than almost all humans at almost all things is most likely in 2026 or 2027 ([his essay](https://darioamodei.com/on-deepseek-and-export-controls)). Musk said in January 2026 that AI might be smarter than any human by the end of that year, "no later than next year" ([at Davos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IgifEgm1-e0)). Suleyman said in February 2026 that most white-collar tasks would be fully automated within 12 to 18 months ([to the Financial Times](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YTrBz6Z5c0E)). Shane Legg of [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/) guessed in December 2025 that what he calls minimal AGI is "probably about two" years away ([the podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l3u_FAv33G0)). Against them, Yann LeCun said in September 2026 that machines smarter than people in every field are coming, but not next year ([at Sciences Po](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y4s8NadbZfU)). Their dates pass at the end of 2027.

**Also in 2027.** Musk said in April 2026 that Tesla's Optimus robot would probably be doing useful work outside Tesla sometime in 2027 ([Tesla's call](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qO7T5zgRvXM)). Tesla has moved its Optimus dates before: in October 2025 Musk said the third version would probably be unveiled in the first quarter of 2026 ([the record](/claims/#2025-10-22-musk-optimus-v3-unveil-q1)). Robots doing paid work are shown beside the economy part of the test, and not scored.
