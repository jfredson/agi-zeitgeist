In February 2026, by its own count, the AI company [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) had its AI leading less than 1 percent of the work of building its next AI. By August the figure was 26 percent ([Anthropic's measurements](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)). Nothing else this site follows moved as far this year, and it moved the part of my test that is closest to its line.

That test asks whether we are entering the [technological singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity), the point where machines improve so quickly that nobody can keep up. It has four parts, and all four have to cross a line fixed in advance and stay there for a year. At the end of 2026 the three parts that can be measured stand, on average, 32 percent of the way to their lines, and the answer is still "Not yet" ([where the test stands](/)). Here is what moved, what did not, and what 2027 could settle.

## AI started doing the research

One part of the test asks whether AI now does most of the work of making AI better. The only numbers anyone has come from the companies themselves, and Anthropic's are the clearest: if its AI leads half of the work, that part counts. At 26 percent it is 52 percent of the way there, and if the trend of 2026 holds, it crosses in 2027 ([where the test stands](/)).

The obvious doubt is that only the company can check its own count. In November an outside group came closest to checking it. [METR](https://metr.org/), a research group that tests AI systems, spent from August 12th to October 29th, 2026 studying Anthropic from the inside. Most of its staff concluded that Anthropic's AI was improving about twice as fast as it had in 2024 and 2025, and that perhaps 80 percent of that speed-up came from the company using its own AI in its research ([METR's report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)). The forecaster Ajeya Cotra argued that this is a much bigger deal than it sounds, and [I wrote about her case in November](/news/2026-11-13-cotra-ai-nearly-doubling-ai-progress/). METR's report measures how fast the research goes, not how much of it AI does, so it does not change the number. It does make the company's number harder to wave away.

## The best AI outgrew the test that timed it

Another part asks whether AI is getting better faster and faster. It is scored on METR's measure of how long a job, in a skilled person's time, the best AI can finish on its own half the time. In April 2026 an early version of Anthropic's Claude Mythos reached about 17 hours, past the 16 hours METR says its tasks can measure reliably, and in September METR stopped updating the series ([METR's page](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). Over the last year it covers, that length doubled every 121 days. That is fast, but this part needs the doubling itself to keep getting quicker, down to 30 days, and over the last year it did not, so the part stands at 43 percent of the way ([where the test stands](/)).

So the measure I chose for this part ran out before the year did. I asked METR in October whether a replacement is planned and have had no answer. Choosing what takes its place is my decision, and the rules I set before the series stopped say any replacement has to be shown running side by side with the old one before it counts. I will write about it when I decide.

## The economy did not change gear

The last part is measured by people outside the AI world: whether the world economy grows far faster than it ever has. The line is 30 percent growth in a year. In 2025 the world economy grew 2.9 percent, about what it has done for decades ([the World Bank's figures](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG?locations=1W)). That part is at zero, and it is why the answer is "Not yet" however fast the other parts move. The fourth part, whether AI has become impossible to predict, still cannot be measured, because nobody has gathered the record of old forecasts it needs ([where the test stands](/)).

## What the leaders said

The talk ran well ahead of the measures. Of the 12 heads of AI companies on this site who have put a year on AGI (a machine that can do most of the thinking work a person can) or something beyond it, 8 name a year before 2030, the middle year of the latest guesses from the long-time thinkers on my panel ([the leaders' predictions](/claims/)). In August, Sam Altman of [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI) told a reporter that by the end of 2026 the company would have an internal system he would call AGI ([TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/08/26/openai-sam-altman-interview/)). In July, 1,386 employees of frontier AI companies asked the US government to support an international effort to build the means to deliberately set the pace of AI that develops AI ([their statement](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/)). And on September 29th, 2026, the White House told federal agencies to write "Super Intelligence" where they used to write "Artificial Intelligence" ([the order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/)).

## What the site found and changed

This site opened on September 30th, 2026. On October 8th I fixed the test and every line in it before scoring anything. On October 17th the number at the top became the average of how far each part has come, with the slowest named, and on October 19th a page began recording what the fifteen people who run the leading AI companies and chipmakers predict, beside what the long-time thinkers say. From October 26th every one of the 37 experts I follow has been searched for new statements every week. Each change, with its date and its reason, is in [the story so far](/about/).

## Coming up in 2027

**January 1st.** The year closes on 29 predictions on the leaders' page whose date is the end of 2026 ([the table](/data/claims.csv)). Among them: Dario Amodei of Anthropic said in February 2025 that by the end of 2026 AI might be close to the best human programmers at everything in coding ([Hard Fork](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhGUSIvsn_Y)); Mustafa Suleyman of Microsoft AI said in December 2025 he was "pretty certain" people would be buying Christmas presents through an AI agent by Christmas 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MUEKVoeeRoA)); and Elon Musk said in July 2024 that Tesla would supply its Optimus robots to outside customers in 2026 ([Tesla's call](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ktkCRVxTuEI)). Each gets a short note saying what happened.

**January.** The World Bank's [Global Economic Prospects](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/global-economic-prospects), published twice a year, should give its first estimate of how fast the world economy grew in 2026. That is the first sign of whether the slowest part moved at all.

**Sometime in 2027.** Anthropic says it will keep publishing its measurements, with no date given ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)). If its AI leads half of the research, that part of the test crosses its line, the first part to do so.

**April 26th to 28th.** The [International Conference on Learning Representations](https://iclr.cc/), one of the largest AI research conferences, holds its 2027 meeting, the first at which every paper must say how AI was used in the research ([its dates](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/Dates), [its rule](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors)). If anyone counts those statements, it could be the first measure of AI doing research that does not rest on a company's word.

**All year.** 2027 is the year named in some of the boldest predictions on record. Amodei wrote that AI smarter than almost all humans at almost all things is most likely in 2026 or 2027 ([his essay](https://darioamodei.com/on-deepseek-and-export-controls)). Musk said in January 2026 that AI might be smarter than any human by the end of that year, "no later than next year" ([at Davos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IgifEgm1-e0)). Suleyman said in February 2026 that most white-collar tasks would be fully automated within 12 to 18 months ([to the Financial Times](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YTrBz6Z5c0E)). Shane Legg of [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/) guessed in December 2025 that what he calls minimal AGI is "probably about two" years away ([the podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l3u_FAv33G0)). Against them, Yann LeCun said in September 2026 that machines smarter than people in every field are coming, but not next year ([at Sciences Po](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y4s8NadbZfU)). Their dates pass at the end of 2027.

**Also in 2027.** Musk said in April 2026 that Tesla's Optimus robot would probably be doing useful work outside Tesla sometime in 2027 ([Tesla's call](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qO7T5zgRvXM)). Tesla has moved its Optimus dates before: in October 2025 Musk said the third version would probably be unveiled in the first quarter of 2026 ([the record](/claims/#2025-10-22-musk-optimus-v3-unveil-q1)). Robots doing paid work are shown beside the economy part of the test, and not scored.
