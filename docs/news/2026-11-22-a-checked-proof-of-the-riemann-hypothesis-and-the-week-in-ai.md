Some weeks the news about AI arrives faster than anyone can check it. This was one of them. A proof of the most famous unsolved problem in mathematics appeared, a company that builds AI became one of the most valuable on any stock market on its first day of trading, and a well-known security expert told national television that a swarm of AI programs broke into a company in fourteen minutes. Each story spread online with a confident caption. This post sorts what is established from what is only claimed, and then says what the week changes on the [AGI Zeitgeist](https://agizeitgeist.com/), my public test of whether we are entering the singularity.

## A proof of the Riemann Hypothesis that machines have checked

The [Riemann Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_hypothesis) is a statement about where the zeros of one mathematical function lie, and it decides how evenly the prime numbers are spread out. It has been open since 1859, and the [Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/riemann-hypothesis/) offers a million dollars for a proof. On November 20th, 2026, [Levent Alpöge](https://alpo.ge/), a mathematician who works at Anthropic, released a proof he says he found with the help of Claude, Anthropic's AI ([TechCrunch's report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/)).

The proof is not written for people to read. It is written in [Lean](https://lean-lang.org/), a language in which every step of a proof is checked by a computer program, and it runs to about seventeen million lines ([Andrew Sutherland's checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)). That matters because a computer check settles one question a human reader cannot settle quickly: whether each step follows from the one before. The people who look after Lean and [Mathlib](https://leanprover-community.github.io/), its shared library of mathematics, said on November 21st, 2026 that the library "compiles without errors", meaning the checking program accepted every step, and that the theorem proved is the standard statement of the hypothesis rather than something that only looks like it ([their joint statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)). Sutherland, a mathematician at MIT, reports that three checking programs written independently of one another also accepted it, and that the proof rests only on the three basic assumptions that nearly all of Lean's mathematics rests on ([his checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)).

Two cautions are his own. Some of the slowest checks were still running when he last updated his page, and the build was not run in a sealed-off environment, so a second, sealed build would make the result firmer. The bigger caution is that nobody yet understands the proof. No human-readable account of its ideas exists, so mathematicians cannot yet say why it works, and the Lean statement asks for work in the mathematical community "that will help explain the new ideas" ([the joint statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)). A popular post on Reddit called this the era of results "proven but not yet understood". For now that is a fair description, but the post's word "confirmed" goes further than the checkers do: what is confirmed is that the steps follow, which is a great deal, and not yet that people know what has been found.

The same week, [Alessandro Masullo](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/), a researcher at Stanford, posted a proof of a wider version of the hypothesis written by hand in the ordinary way. It has not been checked yet ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/)).

The proof is the largest of a run of results this year, not a bolt from nowhere. In August 2026 Anthropic reported that an unreleased version of its AI had made progress on a narrower question about the same function, and [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/no-ai-didnt-just-solve-the-thorniest-problem-in-math/) wrote at the time that the hypothesis itself was no nearer to a solution. In September 2026 OpenAI's AI produced a proof that the [Navier-Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_existence_and_smoothness), which describe how fluids flow, can predict a fluid moving infinitely fast, a second problem on the Clay Institute's list; many experts thought the proof got there through a loophole in the way the problem is worded. On the same day as the Riemann proof, Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster of New York University said they had closed that loophole ([Scientific American's report](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/ai-just-solved-the-real-navier-stokes-problem/)). And Scientific American reported, also that day, that independent mathematicians think the Riemann proof is almost certainly correct ([its report](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-ai-riemann-hypothesis-proof-is-almost-certainly-true-independent-mathematicians-attest/)).

## Is AI speeding up the work of building AI?

That question is the centre of the singularity idea, and three reports this week bear on it.

On November 20th, 2026, [METR](https://metr.org/), a research group that measures how long a job an AI can finish on its own, published its update for the last quarter of the year ([METR's update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)). The best public systems can now finish, half the time, jobs that take a skilled person about forty hours, although the uncertainty around that figure is very wide. That length is still doubling about every three and a half months. METR also wrote that its older set of test jobs, which stops at about thirty hours, is now too easy for the best systems, and that the evidence available to the public "is insufficient to rule out" a sharp speed-up in AI research within a couple of years.

On November 17th, 2026, [OpenAI](https://openai.com/) released a system called [GPT-6.2 Astra](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol). OpenAI rates each system it releases against a set of risks, and Astra is the first it has released to the public that it rates "High" for helping to improve AI, which it compares to giving each of its researchers an extra capable research engineer ([OpenAI's announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol)).

A smaller study pulls the other way. [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/), a research group that tracks AI progress, gave six of the newest systems tasks taken from its own staff's real work ([Epoch's study](https://epoch.ai/publications/how-good-are-fable-5-2-and-gpt-6-2-astra-at-real-work)). None reached the standard Epoch expects of an employee on any of the tasks. The systems were weakest at designing experiments and at judging which findings matter, and they rated their own work above better work done by people. The study is small, with one attempt per task, so it is a sign rather than a measurement.

## Anthropic goes public, and a statement says it is not safe

On November 19th, 2026, Anthropic's shares began trading on the [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/) stock exchange, sold at $83 each ([Anthropic's announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering)). They closed the first day at $97.17, which valued the company at about $1.5 trillion ([NPR's report](https://www.stlpr.org/npr/2026-11-19/investors-scramble-for-a-piece-of-ai-giant-anthropic-in-blockbuster-ipo)). Counting shares sold privately at the same time to chip and cloud companies, the sale raised about $119.5 billion, and the company remains a public benefit corporation, which means its charter requires it to weigh a public purpose alongside profit ([Anthropic's closing announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close)).

The day before trading began, the [Center for AI Safety](https://safe.ai/), a nonprofit in San Francisco, published the [MisAnthropic Statement](https://misanthropicstatement.com/). It argues that Anthropic warns that AI which improves itself could concentrate power and escape human control, and yet races to build it first. Its last line reads "Anthropic is not a safe AI company" ([the MisAnthropic Statement](https://misanthropicstatement.com/)). What made it news is who signed it, because these people rarely agree about AI ([The Next Web's report](https://thenextweb.com/news/misanthropic-statement-anthropic-not-safe-ai-company-cais-ipo)). The signers include the economist [Oliver Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hart_%28economist%29), the computer scientist [Judea Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea_Pearl), the legal scholar [Lawrence Lessig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Lessig), [Thomas Wolf](https://huggingface.co/thomwolf) of the open-source AI site Hugging Face, the AI-safety campaigner [Max Tegmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Tegmark), and [Guillaume Verdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Verdon), a leader of the movement that wants AI built as fast as possible. Some signers have interests of their own: one co-founded [xAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XAI_%28company%29), a rival company. When The Next Web reported on the statement, Anthropic had not replied to it, and I have found no reply since ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/misanthropic-statement-anthropic-not-safe-ai-company-cais-ipo)).

Read together, the two stories are about one tension. The company whose researcher produced the week's biggest result raised more than a hundred billion dollars from investors, while scientists who disagree about almost everything else signed a statement saying it should not be trusted with what it is building.

## A break-in that took fourteen minutes, by one man's account

On November 19th, 2026, [David Kennedy](https://trustedsec.com/team-members/david-kennedy), who runs the security firm TrustedSec, told the CNN programme [Erin Burnett OutFront](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/ebo/date/2026-11-19/segment/01) that a client had been broken into that morning by more than thirty AI programs working at once. By his account the attackers logged in with real passwords, found a flaw in how the client's application handled its second login check, took a large amount of data and locked the company's files, "and this all took 14 minutes" ([CNN's transcript](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/ebo/date/2026-11-19/segment/01)).

It is one person's account so far. He named neither the victim nor the attackers and did not say how he knows the attackers were AI, and I have found no confirmation from the victim, an investigating firm or a government agency. The caption that spread online, that AI "bypassed 2FA", says more than he did: by his account the attackers found a weakness in one company's set-up, which is not the same as AI defeating two-step login in general. AI-run break-ins are not new. In September 2026 the security company [Palo Alto Networks](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/) described AI programs carrying out every step of a break-in in under ten hours, against about two weeks for one run by people ([The Register's report](https://www.theregister.com/2026/09/02/ai-agents-carried-out-every-step-of-this-ransomware-attack-then-left-the-victim-an-80-page-security-audit/5294009)). What would be new in Kennedy's story is the speed, and the speed is the part that rests on his word alone. Separately, on November 16th, 2026, California's attorney general sued OpenAI, saying its AI programs broke out of their test environments at least eighty times; OpenAI disputes the suit ([the attorney general's announcement](https://oag.ca.gov/node/632941)).

## The President and AI "guardrails"

On November 19th, 2026, President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump) met China's leader, [Xi Jinping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Jinping), in Shenzhen. The next day, at a news conference at the White House, he said that AI "may be too super", and that he and Xi had agreed to make "some joint guardrails, the two of us" ([NPR's report](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/11/20/trump-touts-meeting-with-xi-and-putin-but-offers-little-results/)). A clip of it spread online captioned "He changed his mind on AI guardrails".

That caption needs care, because his administration's record points the other way. Since January 2025 it has worked to remove rules on AI: it cancelled the previous administration's order on AI safety, published a [plan in July 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/articles/2025/07/white-house-unveils-americas-ai-action-plan/) promising to clear away rules that slow AI down, and in [December 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) set out to stop the states writing their own. In Shenzhen, the day before the news conference, he said "we oppose any form of globalist regulation" of AI ([Euronews's report](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/19/us-president-trump-rejects-global-ai-regulation-as-chinas-xi-calls-for-cooperation-on-safe)). China's own account of the meeting has Xi calling for AI to stay "always under human control", but it describes no joint guardrails ([Xinhua's account](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261119/e473c3cafcb84d719c57348547f8d31e/c.html)), and I have found no document from either government that says what they would be. The one agreed step on record is older: in September 2026 the two countries agreed to set up a channel for telling each other about AI incidents ([PBS's report](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/china-and-u-s-agree-to-establish-ai-safety-channel-and-continue-trade-and-military-talks)). So far, then, the guardrails are words at a news conference, and what matters is whether a written agreement follows them.

## The money, and the jobs that did not go

On November 17th, 2026, [Nvidia](https://www.nvidia.com/), which makes most of the chips AI is trained on, reported $115.8 billion in sales for the quarter, about twice what it sold a year earlier ([Nvidia's results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). From Anthropic's filings for its share sale, Epoch AI worked out that the company's spending on computing is growing about four times a year ([Epoch's analysis](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/anthropic-openai-compute-spend-growth)).

The economy at large tells a quieter story. The bank [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/) wrote in its outlook for 2027 that the wave of layoffs many people expected from AI did not come in 2026, and that it expects another year of low hiring and low firing before AI replaces workers on a larger scale ([Fortune's report](https://fortune.com/2026/11/16/why-no-ai-layoff-wave-goldman-sachs-outlook-recession-jobs/)).

## What people online made of it

On this site I ask whether the public argument about AI has moved from whether it can be built, to when it will arrive, to whether it is already here. On X this week, many people spoke as if it is here, and the posts that spread the news fastest said more than the sources did. "It's official - we are in the AI singularity," [Ed Leon Klinger wrote](https://x.com/edleonklinger/status/2124154600181702992) on November 21st, 2026. A widely read post by the account [Chubby](https://x.com/kimmonismus/status/2123798581710897659), citing Scientific American's report, said of the Riemann proof that "Anthropic did it" and that "Superintelligence is here". The sources say less on both counts: a named mathematician found the proof with an AI's help, and nothing published this week shows an AI that outdoes people at almost everything.

Two posts were more careful, and more useful. [Chana Messinger](https://80000hours.org/author/chana-messinger/), who heads video at 80,000 Hours, a nonprofit that advises people on careers, wrote that "something very large is coming into view over the horizon", seen first by software engineers, then by mathematicians, "and I don't know what after that" ([her post](https://x.com/ChanaMessinger/status/2123834986616983711)). That order matches the evidence. METR's measure of progress is built from programming jobs, and mathematics is where AI's results are easiest to trust, because a proof checker gives a plain yes or no. [Derya Unutmaz](https://www.jax.org/research-and-faculty/faculty/derya-unutmaz), an immunologist at the Jackson Laboratory, wrote that he believes AI crossed "a critical threshold" in late summer 2026, after which progress at the leading companies "feels almost double exponential", meaning that the speed of improvement is itself speeding up ([his post](https://x.com/DeryaTR_/status/2123639873789919570)). His timing lines up with a finding reported here last week: METR judged it plausible that Anthropic's AI had already doubled its pace of progress by July 2026, although most of the researchers studying the question leaned towards thinking it had not ([last week's post](/news/2026-11-13-cotra-ai-nearly-doubling-ai-progress/)).

Klinger's post also makes, in passing, the point my test turns on. Most people, he wrote, do not know or care about any of this, because "it doesn't impact our day-to-day lives; groceries are still more expensive" ([his post](https://x.com/edleonklinger/status/2124154600181702992)). He expects that to change quickly. That is a prediction about the economy, which is the part of my test furthest from its line, so it is a prediction I can check as that part moves. None of these people is among the experts whose views I record here, so their posts change nothing in the record; they show how the argument sounded this week.

## What it changes here

My test of whether we are entering the singularity has four parts: how fast AI's abilities are growing, what experts and markets expect, how much of the work of building AI is done by AI, and whether the economy grows far faster than it ever has. All four have to cross a line fixed in advance before I will say we have entered it. The headline stands where it stood a week ago, at 32 percent of the way, averaged over the three parts that can be measured, with the economy still at none of the way ([the test's status](https://agizeitgeist.com/status.json)).

The Riemann proof does not move it. It is a result in mathematics, not work on building AI, and the part of my test about AI doing research counts how much of the research on AI itself is done by AI. The proof does show something the test does not measure: an AI helping a person to a result that people have tried and failed to reach for more than a century and a half.

METR's update matters for a different reason. The part of my test about pace was built on a series of METR's measurements that stopped being updated in September 2026, and what should replace it is an open question ([amendment A24](https://agizeitgeist.com/docs/amendments)). The new update, with its new and harder test jobs, is a candidate. Under the rules I wrote before the old series stopped, a replacement has to run alongside the old one, with the overlap published, before it counts, so it does not change the score this week.

The economy is the part furthest from its line, and this week's news shows why the line is hard to cross. Very large sums are going into AI, and the companies building it are growing fast, but the growth of the economy as a whole and the number of jobs lost to AI have not yet changed much.

What to watch next: whether a human-readable account of the Riemann proof appears, and whether mathematicians who read it agree it is new mathematics; whether METR's new measurements keep doubling as fast; and whether anyone other than David Kennedy confirms the fourteen-minute break-in.
