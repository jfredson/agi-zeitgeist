### Murray Shanahan: C2 (a statement on whether or when AGI arrives, dated 2024 or later)

**Verdict on C2: PASS (thin).** Overall: **PASS (thin)**.

- **Counted: 2025-04-24.** Google DeepMind: The Podcast, "Consciousness, reasoning and the
  philosophy of AI | Murray Shanahan", hosted by Hannah Fry. Published on the official
  Google DeepMind YouTube channel on 2025-04-24 (upload date read from the video's own
  record): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1Py_hWcmkU. Source: the channel's uploaded
  English captions (speaker-labelled, so not machine captions), at about 27:00.
  **Primary** (his own spoken words).

  Hannah Fry asks whether good embodied AI would bring "a big up-step in intelligence". He
  answers:

  > "Well, I think it might make a big difference because the large language models we
  > have at the moment, it's really difficult to discern, actually, to be honest, right
  > now, where the limits are for how good they're going to get, whether we really are on
  > the road to producing general intelligence that is comparable to human general
  > intelligence. [...] But it may be that that sort of general ability to really get
  > things on a deep level, on a deep, common sense level, maybe, that that does still
  > require a bit of embodiment."

  Earlier in the same episode (about 2:50): "Now, we don't know quite how things are going
  to unfold in the next few years because maybe robotics will progress rapidly as well in
  the way that language has in AI."

- **Why thin.** It is a statement on *whether* human-level general intelligence is coming
  (he says it is hard to tell whether current systems are on the road to it, and suggests
  a physical body may be needed), but it gives no date, and it is partly about the route.
  Under the codebook (A15 ruling 4) a route claim alone would be coded `unexpressed` for
  endorsement; here he also says the outcome itself is uncertain, which is why it is
  counted. The same footing as Bostrom's 2026 paper and Chalmers's 2026 talk, which pass
  thin on statements without a dated forecast.

- **Checked and not counted: 2024-07-14.** Machine Learning Street Talk, "There are
  monsters in your LLM. (Murray Shanahan)", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ztNdagyT8po
  (upload date 2024-07-14 from the video's record; automatic captions). At about 2:02:00
  he says today's language models "pass the spirit of the Turing test" because "they do
  really have human level conversational skills" and have "attained more or less human
  level language skills". That is about conversation, one capability, not general
  intelligence (the distinction A3 was written for), so it is not used for C2.
- C1 and C3 unchanged from 2026-10-02 (C1: 2014-07 secondhand, with the firmer Guardian
  2015-06-26 quote; C3: Guardian 2015-06-26, 2015-12-27, 2016-03-05 under A16).
- Quota tags unchanged: `frontier_lab` (DeepMind from 2017).

Queries: `Murray Shanahan Google DeepMind podcast Hannah Fry 2025 AGI transcript`;
`Murray Shanahan Machine Learning Street Talk 2024 "AGI" transcript`; `"Murray Shanahan"
"artificial general intelligence" OR "AGI" 2025 OR 2026 interview says`; YouTube search
`Murray Shanahan Machine Learning Street Talk` (through `yt-dlp`). The yt.chop.dev

### Yoshua Bengio: C1 (a statement on whether or when AGI arrives, dated 2017 or earlier)

**Verdict on C1: PASS (thin).** Overall: **PASS (thin)**.

- **Counted: 2017-05 (reported 2017-06-02).** Synced, "C2 Montreal Master Class with Yoshua
  Bengio: Know the Basics", by Meghan Han, 2017-06-02, reporting his master class at the
  C2 Montréal conference, 23 to 25 May 2017:
  https://syncedreview.com/2017/06/02/c2-montreal-master-class-with-yoshua-bengio-know-the-basics/
  (fetched; quote checked in the raw page text).

  > "We are very far from human level intelligence," said Professor Bengio. He stressed
  > however that even if AI research were to stop today, previous discoveries would still
  > provide benefits for another decade.

  Explicitly about human-level intelligence and dated inside the window. **Secondhand**
  (his words quoted by a technology news site), which is why the pass is thin, the same
  footing as Ng's secondhand C1.

- **Also found: the original 2016 interview.** MIT Technology Review, "Will Machines
  Eliminate Us?", by Will Knight, dated January 29, 2016. The old address was found in
  the Wayback Machine's index (`technologyreview.com/s/546301/will-machines-eliminate-us/`,
  snapshots from 2016-02-01) and read in full:
  https://web.archive.org/web/20160201194619/https://www.technologyreview.com/s/546301/will-machines-eliminate-us/.
  **Primary** (question-and-answer interview). Standfirst: "People who worry that we're on
  course to invent dangerously intelligent machines are misunderstanding the state of
  computer science." The introduction frames it around "the progress AI is making toward
  matching, or perhaps surpassing, human intelligence". His words:

  > "companies want to tell a nice little story that 'Oh, we have this revolutionary
  > technology that's going to change the world—AI is almost here, and we are the company
  > that's going to deliver it.' That's not at all how it works."

  Asked "What approaches beyond deep learning will be needed to create a true machine
  intelligence?": "Traditional endeavors, including reasoning and logic—we need to marry
  these things with deep learning in order to move toward AI."

  > "The thing I'm more worried about, in a foreseeable future, is not computers taking
  > over the world. I'm more worried about misuse of AI."

  On its own this interview is close to explicit (it rejects "AI is almost here" in an
  interview about machines matching human intelligence) but never uses "human-level" or
  "general" in his own words. It is kept as the primary backing; the 2017 quote is the
  explicit one.

- **Skeptic tag:** still a near miss, not proposed. "Very far from" gives no timescale, so
  it does not meet "reject or doubt AGI within decades" as written. Recorded for John.
- Not usable: an interview on a Harvard blog with the famous "It could be ten years, it
  could be 100 years" line is dated 2019-06-17 (interview 2019-06-07), too late:
  https://archive.blogs.harvard.edu/toshietakahashi/?p=1291 (fetched). A RE•WORK video
  interview of 2016-08-11 has no transcript on the page.
- C2 and C3 unchanged (C2: 2025-06-03 LawZero post; C3: NYT 2016-07-17, 2017-04-09,
  2017-10-22 under A16). Quota tag `non_us_uk` (Montréal) unchanged.

Queries: `Yoshua Bengio 2016 OR 2017 "human-level" AI interview decades away`; `Bengio
2017 "human-level intelligence" OR "human-level AI" "far from" Montreal interview`;
Wayback Machine index lookup for `technologyreview.com/s/5463*`.
