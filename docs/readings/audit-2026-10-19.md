## The two flagged items, as rulings for me

**1. A second Anthropic figure for how much of its own work AI does.** Anthropic's page "When AI
builds itself" (opened for this audit) says that "as of May 2026, more than 80% of the code we
merge into Anthropic's codebase was authored by Claude", and that in a March 2026 poll of 130
research staff the median person estimated about four times the output with the company's
newest model. The scored measure for the "AI doing the improving" part stays the company's
automation index (26 percent of research work led by its model, August 2026), as I ruled on
2026-10-17.

- **The question:** should the 80 percent code share be recorded beside that part, shown but not
  scored?
- **Recommended: yes**, as a row of the same kind as Google's "75 percent of new code" figure,
  which the record already shows beside that part and does not score. A company's share of code
  written by AI is a proxy for its share of research work at best, and the definition already
  says so. The four-times poll would not be recorded: it measures output, not a share.
- **The alternative:** record nothing, on the ground that three figures from one company already
  sit beside the gauge.

**2. Does a bill that has only been introduced count as a government action?** Senator Bernie
Sanders and Representative Greg Casar introduced a bill in September 2026 that would ban
superintelligence and create a Department of AI. The record's government actions are orders,
laws and declarations that took effect.

- **The question:** does an introduced bill go into the events list?
- **Recommended: no.** A bill enters the list when it passes a chamber of a legislature or
  becomes law, dated by the legislature's own record. Introduced bills are statements of intent
  by their sponsors and would flood the list. This bill stays out for now, and the reading keeps
  watching it.
- **The alternative:** record introduced bills as public statements by their sponsors, dated
  from the legislature's own record.

## Proposed changes to the procedure

1. **Open the primary page for anything flagged.** A flagged item names the record that would
   settle it and opens it (for a bill, the legislature's own register, such as congress.gov),
   so the ruling put to me rests on the record, not a news report. *Applied in the same commit
   as this audit (a sentence in step 3 of `docs/weekly-reading-prompt.md`); a procedure detail,
   not a method change.*
2. **An aggregator is not a source.** For an AI release or any dated event, the date comes from
   the maker's or the institution's own announcement; an aggregator may point the way but is
   never the source cited. *Applied in the same commit (a sentence in step 3).*
3. **Nothing else.** The run already does the two rules The Upgrade Tree's audit asked for: it
   opened the source of every row it added, and it aimed one search at each pollster's own site.
