The rules being checked

tier rule
Core (about 25) C1 a public statement on whether or when AGI arrives dated 2017 or earlier; C2 one dated 2024 or later; C3 named in three distinct pieces about where AI is going, in national outlets, dated 2017 or earlier
2020s cohort (10 to 12) C1 no public AGI timeline statement found before 2018; C2 one dated 2024 or later. C3 was collected but only as information (see the note under the cohort table)
Wide list (60 to 100) every person in the MIRI/AI Impacts predictions dataset and on the TIME100 AI lists 2023 to 2025, minus core and cohort candidates

Core quotas (minimums among those frozen): 6 skeptic (pre-2017 statements reject or doubt AGI within decades), 3 non_us_uk (based outside the United States and United Kingdom when making their pre-2017 statements), 2 philosopher (philosopher or cognitive scientist), 3 frontier_lab (led, ran, co-founded or held a senior research role at a company building the most capable models of its time).

Verdicts: PASS; PASS (thin) = meets the rule only on weak evidence, named in the notes; UNCLEAR = depends on a ruling or a missing check; FAIL = does not meet the rule as searched. Nothing thin is rounded up to a plain pass.

How far to trust this

  • Blocked outlets. The search and page-reading tools could not reach the New York Times, the Guardian, The New Yorker, Wired, the BBC, the Economist, the Financial Times, the Wall Street Journal, The Atlantic or New Scientist. Pieces from them are marked "seen in search result" or were read in copies elsewhere. Several C3 failures are probably the tools, not the record, especially for UK-based candidates (Shanahan, Wooldridge) and Pinker. A library database check would settle them.
  • "Seen in search result" items count toward C3 here. They were not opened; the search showed the outlet, the date and the name.
  • One copy carries four passes. The 2015 New Yorker profile "The Doomsday Invention" was read in full from a course-page printout. It names Kurzweil, Bostrom, Hassabis and Hinton, and counts toward all four.
  • Page reading went through a summarising tool. Quotes should be checked against the live page before going into data/statements.csv.
  • Earlier in the session one helper fetched on-site search pages after the search limit ran out; nothing in these tables depends on them.

Rulings applied (A14, 2026-10-01)

  1. Self-written pieces count toward the three national pieces, but for at most one of the three. This turns Marcus, Russell and Brooks into passes, and confirms Tegmark.
  2. Skeptic window is "2017 or earlier". Kai-Fu Lee gains the tag ("hundreds of years, if ever", 2017-06-24); LeCun's tag is strengthened by "still quite far from building artificial general intelligence" (2017, lecun-2017-01).
  3. Co-written statements count for C2 when the person is a named author: Davis and Brynjolfsson meet C2 on co-written items.
  4. Dennett is dropped from the core; he is added to the wide list.
  5. Tags. frontier_lab applies if true at the time of any statement in the record, with dates in the justification. philosopher means primary role: Hinton and Hassabis are not tagged. Chalmers is non_us_uk. Suleyman is not a skeptic. Under A15 ruling 8, Kurzweil (Google engineering director from 2012), Chollet (Google senior engineer 2015 to 2024), Kai-Fu Lee (01.AI from 2023) and Karnofsky (OpenAI board 2017 to 2021, Anthropic staff from 2025) are tagged frontier_lab. Kokotajlo (trained in philosophy, works as an AI forecaster) is not tagged philosopher (A15 ruling 8).
  6. Cohort prominence is information only.

Still open: archive checks through the Guardian and New York Times APIs (application programming interfaces, their official data services) once John has keys (ruling 7).

Newspaper archive checks (2026-10-01)

Run with scripts/archive_check.py through the Guardian Open Platform and the New York Times Article Search API (A14 ruling 7, A15). For each person: the name with AI-future terms, pre-2018 (for C3) and 2024 onward (leads for C2). Every hit is in panel-evidence-2026-10/archive-checks.csv (1,184 rows). A hit counts toward C3 only if the person's full name is in the headline or abstract and the headline or abstract carries an AI-future term. Name in the headline with no abstract: flagged headline_only, not counted (3 hits). Name only in the article text: flagged named_in_text_only, not counted (1,037 hits).

The New York Times closed the connection twice. After the first, the run was resumed once; after the second (while checking Brynjolfsson's 2024-onward results) it was stopped, per the overnight rule.
