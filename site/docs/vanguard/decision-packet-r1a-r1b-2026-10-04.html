Decision packet: rules R1a and R1b (posts passed on without comment)

Prepared 2026-10-04 for John, once all three models had coded the trial posts. The recommendations below were the agent's.

Ruled by John, 2026-10-04: R1a adopted with the reactions sentence; R1b rejected. Now in docs/vanguard/codebook.md and docs/amendments.md.

What you are deciding

Two proposed codebook rules, each about posts where someone passes on a news item, a demo video, a product launch or a screenshot of someone else's words and adds nothing of their own. For each: adopt, refine (adopt with a change), or reject.

  • R1a, whether the poster agrees (the stance field): such a post is coded "passes it on without taking a side" (neutral_relay) unless the poster adds words of their own accepting or rejecting the claim. Full text: docs/vanguard/trial-rule-r1a-2026-10-03.md.
  • R1b, whose claim it is (the attribution field): such a post's claim is credited to the source shown (the news outlet, the company, or the person quoted), or to the poster if no source is shown. Full text: docs/vanguard/trial-rule-r1b-2026-10-03.md.

Recommendation

rule recommendation in one line
R1a Refine, then adopt Without it, "whether the poster agrees" fails your reliability test; with it, all three model pairs pass. Add back one sentence on reactions and jokes, which the split dropped.
R1b Reject "Whose claim it is" already passes comfortably without it, and the rule made no measurable difference.

The sentence to add to R1a is already written. It was part of the original combined rule R1 and was lost when R1 was split into R1a and R1b:

Jokes and reaction titles ("WTF", "Shots fired!", an emoji) are not endorsements; code the passed-on claim as neutral_relay and record the tone in rhetorical_mode.

How the trial worked

  • Posts: the same 112 r/singularity posts for every run (data/vanguard/r1-split-trial-posts.csv).
  • Coders: three models from different companies (Claude, Gemini, GPT-6 Astra), each blind to the others' codes, as section 9 now requires.
  • Each rule tested alone. One run had today's codebook plus R1a; a second run had today's codebook plus R1b. Each run is the other's comparison: the R1b run is "today's codebook without R1a" for stance, and the R1a run is "today's codebook without R1b" for attribution. Same posts, same day, same models; only the rule under test differs.
  • Measure: Cohen's kappa for each pair of models, which is agreement corrected for what chance alone would produce (1 is perfect, 0 is chance). Your test: a field is unreliable if every pair is below 0.6, or if no two models agree on more than 15% of posts.
  • How much chance could move the numbers: each comparison rests on about 65 posts that both models in a pair found eligible, so a difference of 0.1 can be noise. The posts were resampled 2,000 times (scripts/vanguard_r1_bootstrap.py); the ranges below are the middle 90% of those resamples.

  • Without R1a the field fails your test under today's codebook: every pair is below 0.6. With R1a every pair is at or above 0.65, and every post has at least two models agreeing (none left unsettled, against 4% without).
  • The gain is clear in the two pairs that include Claude and likely, but not certain, between Gemini and GPT.
  • For reference, under the original codebook (before R2 and R3, run 2026-10-03) the three pairs were 0.42, 0.35 and 0.71.

Where the models still disagree with R1a (14 of 112 posts). About nine of the fourteen are posts whose only words are a reaction or a joke, and the models read them differently:

  • Gemini counts a reaction as agreement where Claude and GPT don't: "HOLY SHIT" over a benchmark chart, "This little shit", "Grok, I wasn't familiar with your game.", "Grok is openly rebelling against its owner".
  • GPT counts a reaction as passing it on where Claude and Gemini read agreement: "This… could be something…", "I'm not even excited for Grok 4 anymore", "AI is coming in fast".

The added sentence speaks to exactly these cases. It has not been tested on its own, so the refined rule's agreement is expected to be at least as good as above but is not measured.

model pair without R1b with R1b change (90% range) R1b higher in
Claude–Gemini 0.77 0.83 +0.06 (−0.07 to +0.20) 77% of resamples

  • The field passes your test either way, with every pair at 0.72 or above.
  • The rule doesn't move agreement: one pair up a little, two down a little, every range spanning zero. Under the original codebook the pairs were 0.72, 0.66 and 0.80.
  • Its remaining disagreements are the same reaction posts ("WTF", "What the hell bruh", "This is objectively hilarious"): the models can't agree on whether a screenshot with a one-word reaction has a source. A rule about sources doesn't settle that; the R1a sentence above may help indirectly.
  • Rejecting R1b leaves attribution coded as the codebook already says, which is how it reached 0.75 or better here.

Cautions

  1. Optimistic by construction. These rules were written from disagreements on these same posts, so they will look better here than on new posts. Section 9 already requires any adopted rule to be checked again on a fresh sample.
  2. Small numbers. About 65 posts per pair. The ranges above say how far that goes: the stance result is solid in two pairs and likely in the third; the attribution result is "no detectable effect", not "proven useless".
  3. The models vary from run to run. Between Gemini and GPT, stance agreement was 0.71 under the original codebook and 0.53 under today's codebook without R1a. Some of that is the R2 and R3 changes, some is chance; the trial design compares runs from the same day to keep it out of the R1a and R1b comparisons.
